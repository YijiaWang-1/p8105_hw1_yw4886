``` r
set.seed(1)
hw_df =
  tibble(
    random_sample = rnorm(10),
    logical_vector = random_sample > 0,
    character_vector = c(
```

Taking the mean works for the numeric and logical variables. The numeric
variable has a mean of 0.1322028, and the logical variable has a mean of
0.6. Taking the mean of the character and factor variables does not work
because these variables are not numeric or logical.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(hw_df, logical_vector))
as.numeric(pull(hw_df, character_vector))
as.numeric(pull(hw_df, factor_vector))
```

Applying as.numeric to the logical vector converts TRUE to 1 and FALSE
to 0, which helps explain why its mean can be calculated. Applying
as.numeric to the character vector does not produce meaningful numeric
values because the character strings are not numbers. Applying
as.numeric to the factor returns the underlying integer codes of its
levels, rather than meaningful numerical measurements. This helps
explain why taking the mean is appropriate for the numeric and logical
variables but not directly meaningful for the character or factor
variables.
